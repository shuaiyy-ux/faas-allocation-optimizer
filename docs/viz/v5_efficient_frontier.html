The Efficient Frontier — 1,024 calibration experiments, one pick

Every dot is one weight combination from our 81,920-simulation sweep, averaged across 80 fleet scenarios. Up and to the left is better (more demand captured, lower total cost). The orange dashed curve is the efficient frontier — 155 combinations that cannot be improved on both benefit and cost simultaneously. From those 155, we pick the one geometrically closest to the "dream corner".
869 dominated combinations 155 on the efficient frontier ⭐ our pick — closest to utopia ✧ utopia (dream corner)
Why this point, out of the 155 on the frontier?

Every dot on the orange curve is Pareto-optimal — no other combination beats it on both benefit and cost. So the choice among those 155 is a question of where on the curve we want to sit, not which curve to chase.

We pick the point whose straight-line distance to the utopia corner (benefit = 1, cost = 0) is shortest — the green dashed line on the chart. This is the frontier point that's simultaneously highest on benefit AND lowest on cost, with no axis over-optimized at the other's expense.

Shifting anywhere else along the curve means either spending more money to buy marginal benefit (right along the curve) or sacrificing disproportionate benefit to save a little cost (left along the curve). The ⭐ is the "balanced" choice — the elbow of the trade-off.
How the two axes are built — without assuming HCA's dollar rates:
  • Benefit (y) = 50 % normalized avg_rented_at_dest (cars currently rented at the destination dealer, RENTED column) + 50 % normalized avg_util_at_dest (utilization rate at the destination dealer). No dollar assumption — both are data columns we have.
  • Cost (x) = 50 % normalized avg_distance (trucking miles) + 50 % normalized avg_annual_tax (property-tax dollars per car per year). Lower = cheaper.
Each raw criterion normalized to [0, 1] against the 1,024- combo observed range. The 2-D projection preserves Pareto dominance; the knee-selection is Euclidean distance to (cost=0, benefit=1).
